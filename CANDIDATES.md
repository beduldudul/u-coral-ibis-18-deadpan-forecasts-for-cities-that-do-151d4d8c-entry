# Candidates — Deadpan Forecasts for Cities That Do Not Exist

Brief: one line, ≤160 chars, weather forecast for a city that does not exist.
Absurd but believable, deadpan TV-meteorologist voice, concrete image/number,
twist lands in the last words. Scored on: concrete + specific / twist in last
words / broadcast tone / exact brief fit / fresh angle.

## 1. Nulberry — fog, alphabetically (8.0/10)
"Good evening from Nulberry, where tonight's fog arrives alphabetically, street by street, and residents are asked not to inhale the letter Q."
Concrete image (alphabetical fog) but the Q joke is a little cute rather than deadpan; twist is mid-sentence, not the last words.

## 2. Port Sallow — penguin mayor (8.8/10)
"Over in Port Sallow, a high of 41 degrees and a low of one very confused penguin, who wandered in during the storm and has now been elected mayor."
Strong concrete number, twist lands at the very end, believable broadcast opening. Slightly long setup; penguin-mayor is a known joke shape.

## 3. Varn — rain of unopened mail (8.4/10)
"In the city of Varn, expect scattered showers of unopened mail from 2pm, with a 90 percent chance your electricity bill finally arrives, soaked."
Vivid and specific (2pm, 90 percent, electricity bill). Twist is good but "soaked" is a small payoff for the setup.

## 4. Drellwick — the moon is a streetlight (7.6/10)
"Tonight in Drellwick: clear skies, a gentle breeze of 12 knots, and a 40 percent chance the moon is just the town's streetlight, switched on early."
Nice deadpan delivery, but the image is thin — one streetlight does not make a city feel real.

## 5. Zamborough — snow of paperwork (8.5/10)
"Zamborough wakes to 6 inches of fresh paperwork, falling steadily since midnight. Commuters are advised to shovel their inboxes before driving."
Fresh metaphor, concrete number, second sentence lands the joke. "Shovel their inboxes" is the kind of line a straight-faced forecaster would actually say.

## 6. Quist — rain that falls upwards (9.0/10) — CHOSEN
"In Quist, the forecast is a high of 19, a low of 4, and a 100 percent chance of rain that falls upwards on Tuesdays. Locals no longer comment on it."
Best deadpan: three hard numbers, one impossible fact stated as routine, and the
kicker ("Locals no longer comment on it") is pure unbothered-meteorologist voice.
The twist is the last sentence and it reframes the whole forecast as normal.

## 7. Trelk — the cloud that won't pay rent (7.8/10)
"Good morning from Trelk: overnight lows of minus 3, with frost on the windshields and the town's single cloud still refusing to pay rent."
Charming, but the cloud joke is the only absurdity and it sits in the middle; the ending fizzles.

## 8. Brindle — snowman files a complaint (8.2/10)
"In the capital of Brindle, expect light snow, heavy silence, and one snowman who has filed a formal complaint about being built without a permit."
Good last-words twist ("without a permit") and a real broadcast cadence, but "heavy silence" is padding and the city feels less real than Quist's numbers.
